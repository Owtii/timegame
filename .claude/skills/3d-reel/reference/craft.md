# Craft: making motion, camera, light and text feel right

Everything here comes from the user's notes (see `feedback.md`). Follow it by default; the user should not have to ask twice. Sound is in `sound.md`, narration in `narration.md`, the look in `style.md`. The exact numbers for all of it (speeds, turn times, camera moves, field of view, light flicker, sizes, colours, detail minimums) are in `rules.md`.

## The three things every note is really about

1. **Smooth.** Nothing snaps, stops dead, jumps or switches abruptly, unless the story calls for a jolt (a slam, a fall, a jump-scare).
2. **Natural.** It moves the way a real person, camera or object would, with weight, reasons and the right speed.
3. **Readable.** From the actual camera, at a glance: lit, not hidden behind something, not cluttered, and matching the caption.

## Pacing at the end of a narrated reel

- **No long silent stretches.** "There are some extra seconds with no narrations and it just weird akward silence with visuals." Once a reel is narrated, a gap longer than about 4 s with no voice reads as dead air. That's true even when the figure is busy, unless a clear event fills the gap (a crash, a reveal).
- **Cut a whole act; never speed things up.** "Just cut some extra acts... dont make anything rushed, let them have natural movements and natural camera movements."
  - Find the act that repeats something: in Part 2 it was a second rage, tearing photos off the far wall, before the desk smash. Remove it whole.
  - Join the two sides with a short, natural move (a walk along the wall).
  - Shift everything after it by the same amount, so every remaining turn, look and camera move keeps its timing.
  - Then re-place the narration so the lines cover the new gaps.

## Camera (first person)

- **One continuous path.** "The camera movement must feel like it has one path and smooth and not switch quickly to another focus of object." Plan a shot's moves as one curve through its targets. Ease every move: the reels average the path with a gaussian over ±0.4 s. Keep snaps for real jolts only (getting up, a slam, a fall), inside marked rush windows.
- **Never through things.** The explainer's opening flew through buildings and they asked for "just show a preview of city then the earthquake. then it shows the humans… then show the cell line tower but all smooth with no weird stops".
- **Not too close.**
  - "Dont zoom in too much on the people, just make it clearly that they are calling."
  - "Dont make the camera too zoomed in when going to the celltower, have a bit space… on the final shot it can be zoomed in."
  - Once they asked for a general 0.9x zoom-out. Push-ins are small (a few degrees) and motivated (fear, focus, a reveal).
- **Not too much looking around.** A scared point of view is a few slow glances with holds and a very slight tremble, not darting.
  - A quick glance needs a short hold, or the smoothing erases it.
  - A string of short darting glances (high, then low, then across) reads as jerky. Pace a look-round as even sweeps (about 120°/s, holds of 0.2–0.3 s), each new target near the last, never a big jump in height.
- **Casual when it's casual.**
  - "Dont make it too sudden the camera movement and dont use jumpscare sound effect, make it more casual the looking around."
  - "Dont keep looking and tracking him… just have a look at him then look forward… then back."
- **Turning round fast while scared** is still a smooth turn, ideally while backing up, so it reads as "backing up and looking behind".
- **Show the way out** when it matters (the open exit beyond the doorway, a green EXIT sign) and zoom on it a little.
- **Camera shake:** only a little, only on impacts ("dont shake too much with the camera just a little bit").
- **Don't turn the camera for the reveal.** "Dont let the camera turn around. freeze the scene and blur it out on the background." Freeze right before the reveal, blur the background, then the question card.
- **Eye-level details:**
  - Through locker slits: narrow gaps, with the eye in the middle of the slits so the room beyond is visible.
  - Hiding behind a bed: peek over it.
  - Reading a phone: the phone held steady ("make the phone less shaky").
- **Never show the player's hands or legs.** Objects react on their own: the speaker key presses down, the door swings shut.
- **Hold a shot for its line.** If narration has to fit, hold the picture a little longer with camera keys rather than rushing the voice.

## People and figures

**Nothing moves "magically".**
- **Turning on the spot:** the feet step round with the body, in small steps that barely leave the floor (no high leg swings). "Make him turn around with his foot too, not just turn around magically." A big turn takes longer: about 0.7 s plus 0.3 s per radian, 0.85–1.4 s in all.
- **The head leads and the body follows a beat later.** "When he turns back to go to the door, make his head turn first then his body." When he looks at something, his head turns first, slowly, then his shoulders and hips come round.
- **Looks are slow, with a hold on each thing.** "Too fast and robotic" is the most common complaint. A "quick look" still takes about a second and is smooth.
- **Never fully still:** breathing (the chest heaves in time with the breathing sound), small weight shifts, a little sway. A figure meant to be "not moving" still has a "very slight movement".
- **Taller than the door:** he ducks and hunches under the frame, head down, slow ("it will make it more scary").
- **Walking:**
  - Realistic, never hopping ("he shouldnt look like he is hopping around").
  - Speed matches stride so feet don't slide, and the walk phase carries across segments.
  - Slow and heavy for menace, fast only when running.

**Poses must read at a glance, from the actual camera.**
- Picking something up: a stoop (knees bent, back well over, one arm down), not a deep frog squat.
- Reading: a sheet held at chest height in both hands, head bowed over it. Hands up at the faceplate read as covering his face.
- Throwing down: a short wind-up at shoulder height. Not an overhead swing.
- Arms straight out in front read as a zombie. Keep elbows bent and arms close unless he's reaching for something.
- Looking down needs a reason: "when looking down, make him do something small so it makes sense", like a pen rolling or writing.

**Every action has a reason the viewer can see, but keep it simple.**
- "He is just standing and hitting something, make it more sense like he is getting angry for a reason."
- At the fallen box he stares at what fell, looks round the room for whoever might be there, then breaks things.
- A paper-reading scene was tried and cut: "he should not pick up things".
- **Breaking things:**
  - Give each blow a clear target you can see from the camera: both hands slammed on the desk edge, hanging over it breathing, one arm sweeping it clear (lamp, phone, papers fly; the mug rolls off and breaks).
  - Then the lockers on the other side of the room, the search coming closer.
  - His breathing gets heavier and faster.
  - No overhead double-fist slams, no flung arms, no jumping-jack sweeps ("dont make him do too weird movements. it feels very robotic").
- **"Really watching stuff"** is a sequence with holds (up the shelf, down at the boxes, leaning in a little), 2 s or more, not one glance.
- **Moving between two close targets** (two lockers): plant him between them and turn on the spot. A side-step walk swings the body to the walking direction and the turn back takes over a second, so blows land mid-turn.
- **Opening something** (a locker, a door): the viewer has to see it open from where they are. Swing it about 100° so it stands out, give the inside its own look (bare, lighter steel), and have him step straight back out of the line of sight.

**Physical sanity.**
- He never walks through furniture. Run the collision check after any path change ("he walks through the desk. fix that, make sure that never happends ever again").
- Hands and gloves never poke through the suit, and the shoulder doesn't twitch. Papers on a desk never flicker through each other, and the desk never moves on its own.
- Keep the important action in the light. If a spot is dark from the camera's view, move the action (or turn him toward the light) rather than adding an unmotivated lamp.
- **Distance:**
  - In the locker scene he stops about 1.2 m from the slits; a suit filling the frame reads as a wall.
  - Where the action happens relative to the viewer matters: "he should be busy with lockers somewhere right of the camera not on the left".
  - Things the viewer must watch go about 10–30° either side of straight ahead and 2–3 m away.
- **True scale:** people smaller than cars, buildings square and real, doors taller than people, the figure taller than the door.

**Figure design** is in `style.md` (the hazmat man, the shadow figure). Rule of thumb: one seamless body, real proportions, no visible joins, nothing like a robot or a doll.

## Explainer visuals (The Day the Phones Jammed)

- **One idea per visual**, understood without the narration: big balls are calls, small particles are texts, a lane is a call path, a scale is the network's capacity.
- **Show the contrast plainly:** a normal day keeps the scale level; everyone calling piles on balls until it overloads and errors. When the network is jammed there are more red (declined) lines than green.
- **Show the change plainly:** "it had to really show that it stops w calling and goes texting". Make the switch a visible event with its own sound. Then some calls still get through, but many texts slip through more easily.
- **Flow without stops:** balls move along lanes with smooth easing and no hitches ("it must have a smooth animation no weird stops").
- **Organised and symmetrical, not chaotic:** lanes evenly spaced, calls in tidy streams ("make everything a bit more organised and symettrical").
- **Particles must say what they are:** "make the particals also more clearly what those are and what they are meant for." Use a different size, colour and shape per meaning, and label them the first time they appear.
- **3D words** punctuate the key moments ("5.8 MAGNITUDE", "CALL FAILED", "FULL", "STABLE", "OVERLOAD"): they arrive with a little motion and catch the light, with no shadow.
- **The phone:** an iPhone with iOS call screens (real accept and decline icons), iMessage bubbles, a clean status bar. Blur the scene behind it while it's on screen.

## Light

- **Contrast:** dark spots darker, bright spots brighter (a luminance S-curve in the grade), on rooms, objects and people.
- **Light tells the story:**
  - A failing tube drops out.
  - A tube jolted by a crash flares and stutters, so the place is seen in flashes.
  - Dead tubes try to strike again.
  - Lights cut off one after another toward you.
  - A dead ceiling tube catches at each of his blows and flares on the freeze frame so his faceplate reads.
  - The bathroom light snaps on mid-sentence.
- **Flicker for meaning:** "make the light flicker to dark and light" in the hallway with the figure, and "it gets a bit darker and light flickers a bit" during the stare.
- **Seen but not known:** a figure meant to be unidentifiable is his own shape in a dark, lit, matte skin with a feathered outline, far back in a dim, flickering room that matches him, still, held a moment while I stare; when the light dies he is gone, and the next flash shows the place empty (v28, see `rules.md` 1.3). Not a flat black cutout under a bright light (v27: "very stupid and ugly").
- **Check the side of him the camera sees.** A doorway light behind him makes him a black shape unless a motivated light (a tube, its bounce off the lockers) reaches his front.
- **Shadows sell entrances:** going through a doorway darkens him ("bring little shadows on it so it really feels like he is entering the door"). The shadow of someone behind you is a real, soft-edged shadow of a person.
- **No unmotivated light:** "dont add extra light because it got more bright on the end."
- **Faces behind glass** are barely visible: foggy, and you can't tell if it's a human face. The face stays blurred everywhere it shows ("but why did his face changed at thr end? it should stay blury").
- **Glow** is soft and wide. A screen's glow is lowered until it no longer blooms over the picture (−15% on the phone).

## Detail

- Small details everywhere:
  - Fine dust drifting only where light hits it (not snow).
  - Grit falling on impacts, contact shadows under furniture and people, dark bands at the foot of walls.
  - Grime, cobwebs, papers on the floor that never z-fight.
- **Debris looks random, never "organized"** ("the partials are weird when they drop from the door its too organized").
  - Spawn from a few crack points in clumps.
  - Mix kinds: heavy grit falling fast with drag, paint flakes fluttering, fine dust hanging.
  - Stagger the release, and vary size and brightness per particle.
  - Lit like everything else, never glowing.
- **Props modelled after real ones:** a jacket with a real collar ("the weird black thing on the green jacket is not correct"), shoes, a mug, wall clocks with centred digits ("it looks a bit off centred the time"), desk phones with lit keys and a display, locker doors with narrow louvres and a stiffener inside.
- **When asked for "more details"** (a building, a hall, a room, a lift), add real-world parts: trim, signs, vents, fixtures, wear and clutter. Keep the composition calm.

## Transitions

- They must read as transitions: smooth, with a blur ("make this transition more blurry and smooth, so you know its a transition").
- An eye-blink closes fully, at the edges too ("it feels he didnt fully closed his eye because the sides are still open but middle closed").
- A time-lapse (hours passing): a radial blur round the edges and a soft sound, with the clock in focus and changing.
- An awkward jump in the story ("at 0:40 the transition is a bit akward") needs a bridge: a sound, a camera move, a blink. Never a bare cut.
- The end: a cut to full black, held about 1 s.

## Captions on screen

- The narrator is plain white. What I say in the moment is italic gold (`who:'me'`). A remembered phone voice is green (`who:'phone'`).
- **Captions never linger past their shot's cut or onto a transition.** The user flagged three in one round.
- A caption matches what's on screen while it shows, stays a little mysterious, and never contradicts the picture (see `story.md`).
- Text placement and size are adjustable per section in the Look and Text studio. Keep captions clear of the platform buttons.
- No stray UI on the picture: they deleted a "Part 1 of 3" tag, a "watch again" text, an info text at the top, and a small camera-corner graphic ("delete the little 90degree thing looking like a camera thing").

## The choice card (end of a series part)

- Two options as **clean matte 3D keycaps**: a flat face on a darker side wall, a soft shadow, light from above.
  - Dark red against dark blue, text only, centred, and "or" as plain text.
  - No glow, no gloss, no pill shapes, no icons or emoji. The user called glossy glowing buttons "very ai design".
- The picked key latches down; the other goes dull but stays solid.
- The background is the frozen last frame, blurred. A quiet horror tone holds under it.
- One question, held about 2 s longer than feels necessary.
- The page draws it in CSS (`choice.css`) and the exported video draws it on canvas (`keycap()` in `choice.js`). Change both together. Design it in `scripts/cardlab/`, which renders in seconds.

## Before you publish

Render the moments you touched, from the real camera and from a lit side view, and run the full test (see `testing.md`).
